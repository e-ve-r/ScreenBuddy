PART A — DIALECTIC OF RESEARCH
Using claims, counterclaims and evidence to refine research
LEARN & REMEMBER
Dialecticis structured reasoning through opposing or different views.
In Computer Science research, the process can be understood as:
Claim→Challenge→Evidence→Refinement
It helps researchers:
•identify weaknesses in an idea,
•consider alternative viewpoints,
•reduce premature conclusions,
•refine vague research ideas,
•strengthen research arguments.
A1. Dialectical Process
1. Identify a claim or hypothesis.
2. Challenge it using counter-arguments or experiments.
3. Examine evidence and logic.
4. Refine the claim.
5. Arrive at a stronger explanation or solution.
PRACTICE ZONE

“Greedy algorithms always produce optimal results.”

PART D — PROOF BY DEMONSTRATION
Examples and prototypes as early evidence
LEARN & REMEMBER
Proof by demonstration means showing that something works using examples, test cases, or a proto-
type.
It is:
•easy to understand,
•useful for early research,
•useful for prototypes,
•useful for teaching,
•but not a universal proof.
D1. Example
Claim:
“This new sorting algorithm is efficient.”
Suppose we run it on five sample arrays and all five executions are successful.
This demonstrates that it works for those tested examples.
It doesnotprove that it is efficient for every possible input.

PART H — DEDUCTION AND INDUCTION
Using evidence and logic to build CS knowledge
LEARN & REMEMBER
Deduction
General rule→Specific case
If the premises are true and the logic is valid, the conclusion follows.
Induction
Specific observations→General rule
The conclusion is probable rather than guaranteed.

Ends with Specific conclusion Generalization Best explanation
