4 Part D — What Can Go Wrong? Scope and Boundaries
Common errors in selecting a research problem
1. Ambiguity / vagueness
The problem does not clearly state what is being
investigated.
2. Too broad
The study becomes unrealistic or unmanageable.
3. Too narrow
There may be insufficient room for meaningful
investigation.
4. Lack of relevance
The problem has weak academic, practical, or
societal value.
5. Lack of feasibility
Resources, data, skills, time, or infrastructure are
unavailable.
6. Ignoring prior art
Existing research may already answer the ques-
tion.

Research objectives:

•help define scope and methodology; and
•provide focus and coherence.
A good objective is not just an activity such as “study AI.” It stateswhat will be achieved or

Activity 11: SMART objective laboratory
Research topic/problem:
Write one objective, then audit it.
Specific What exactly will be done?
Measurable What variable, metric, or evidence will show achievement?
Achievable Why is it realistic with available resources?
Relevant How does it address the research problem?
Time-bound By when / over what period?

8 Part H — From Data to Meaning: Analysis and Interpretation
H1. Do not confuse these stages
Collect
raw evidence
Analyze
organize/compare
Interpret
explain meaning
Conclude
answer problem
Analysisasks: “What patterns, differences, relationships, or measurements are in the data?”
Interpretationasks: “What do those findings mean for my research problem and objectives?”
Activity 15: Read the result, then interpret it
Suppose an experiment gives:
Model Accuracy Inference time (ms)
A 92% 20
B 95% 70
C 94% 25
1. Which model has the highest accuracy?
2. Which model is fastest?
3. If the system is real-time and accuracy must be at least 93%, which model would you investigate first?
Why?
4. Is “B is best” a valid conclusion without considering the research objective? Explain.
